Blog Series

Architecting Intelligence

A ground-up journey through the ideas behind large language models. No hand-waving. Every concept earned from the one before it, starting from raw text and building toward a working transformer.

7 parts published · Series in progress

Published so far
Part 1
Text Was a Problem Long Before AI
From telegraph codes to Unicode. Why representing text is harder than it looks, and why encoding solved storage but not meaning.
Part 2
From Characters to Concepts
Encoding gave words stable identities but no relationships. Learning needs measurable similarity — and text doesn't have it yet.
Part 3
From Words to Spaces
Co-occurrence reveals structure. A tiny corpus shows how meaning appears from counting neighbours — and why the V×V matrix can't scale.
Part 4
From Counting to Learning
Replace the explicit table with two compact matrices. Centre vectors, context vectors, and the factorisation that makes it possible.
Part 5
What the Dot Product Really Measures
The dot product as a compatibility signal. Why two vectors per word, what "better" means for embeddings, and why similar words cluster without being told to.
Part 6
Why Raw Dot Products Aren't Enough
Scores are unbounded, sign-ambiguous, and isolated. Exponentiation fixes sign. Normalisation forces competition. Softmax gives us a probability distribution.
Part 7
Two Distributions, One Goal
The model has beliefs. The data has truth. Training is repeated exposure that turns frequency into geometric pressure.
Where this is going

The first seven parts built the foundation: why text is hard, how co-occurrence encodes meaning, and how raw scores become probability distributions that can be compared to real data.

Next comes learning — cross-entropy as a way to measure wrong beliefs, gradients as a way to fix them, and backpropagation as the machinery that makes it practical. From there, the focus shifts to sequence and attention: why word order matters, how a model decides which words to listen to, and how multiple attention heads capture different kinds of relationships.

The series ends where modern LLMs begin — with the transformer architecture and pretraining on next-token prediction.

Building understanding, one layer at a time.

Part 01 · The limits of gears

Why gears could not take us further.

  • Friction: every gear loses energy. More gears means slower, hotter, weaker.
  • Wear: brass teeth grind down. Precision degrades.
  • Scale: want to add 10 digit numbers? You need 10 gear stacks. 20 digits? Forget it.
  • Speed: physical motion will always be slower than electricity.
The future had to be electronic. No moving parts. Just electrons.

Part 02 · The road not taken
True story

The Soviets tried three states.

The Setun computer in 1958 used ternary: minus one, zero, plus one. Mathematically elegant. Practically, a disaster.

  • Noise: was that 4.7V a "5" or a "4"?
  • Heat shifts voltage. So does humidity.
  • Cost: hard to make millions of components that all agree.

Then check: 0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3   →   false

Your phone, your laptop, NASA's supercomputers, all of them think 0.1 plus 0.2 is not 0.3.

Part 05 · Why it happens

Why?

In decimal, 1 divided by 3 is 0.3333..., infinite, can't be written exactly.

In binary, 0.1 (one tenth) is also an infinite repeating fraction.

0.1 in binary

0.0001100110011001100110011001100110011...

Forever. The pattern 0011 repeats with no end.

Computers have finite memory. They chop it off. That tiny error is real. It is not a bug, it is a fundamental limit of binary.
Part 05 · Case study #1
Case #1 · February 25, 1991 · Dhahran, Saudi Arabia

The Patriot Missile Disaster.

Gulf War. An Iraqi Scud missile is incoming. America's pride, the Patriot defense system, is supposed to shoot it down.

It missed. The Scud hit a US Army barracks.

28 soldiers killed. 100+ injured.

Patriot missile system · Gulf War, 1991
Part 05 · How a tiny number killed

The cause was 0.1.

  • System tracks time in tenths of a second.
  • To get seconds, it multiplies by 0.1.
  • But 0.1 cannot be stored exactly in binary. A tiny rounding error each time.
  • After 100 hours of continuous operation, the clock was off by 0.34 seconds.
  • In 0.34 seconds, a Scud travels half a kilometer.
  • The Patriot looked in the wrong place. The Scud got through.
28 people died because binary cannot store 0.1 exactly.
Part 05 · Case study #2
Case #2 · June 4, 1996 · French Guiana

Ariane 5: half a billion dollars, 37 seconds.

European Space Agency. Maiden flight of a brand new rocket. Years of work. Hundreds of millions of euros. Four scientific satellites on board.

37 seconds in. BOOM.

A 64 bit floating point number was converted to a 16 bit integer. It did not fit.

Ariane 501 · self-destruct, 37 seconds in

They reused code from Ariane 4. The new rocket flew faster. Number got too big. Overflow. Crash.

Part 05 · Case study #3
Case #3 · 1994 · Intel Corporation

Even Intel got it wrong.

A math professor named Thomas Nicely is computing prime numbers on his new Pentium. He gets a wrong answer.

Five entries were missing from a lookup table inside the chip. 1 in 9 billion divisions would be wrong.

Intel said it was fine. Then IBM stopped selling Pentium PCs.

Cost to Intel: $475 M

Part 06 · Case study #4
Case #4 · February 2018 · Apple Inc.

జ్ఞా

One character. Sent over iMessage. Crashed every Apple device that displayed it.

Part 06 · How one character broke a trillion dollar company

What actually happened.

  • iOS 11.2.5. Every iPhone, Mac, iPad, Apple Watch, HomePod.
  • Receive the character, and Messages crashes.
  • Open it again, it crashes again. And again. And again.
  • Display it in a notification, and the entire phone boot loops.
  • Only fix: complete factory reset. All data gone.
  • Trolls on Twitter started spamming it to strangers' phones.
Why? Telugu combines consonants into compound symbols. A specific sequence (consonant + virama + consonant + zero width non joiner + vowel) confused Apple's font renderer.

A trillion dollar company. Defeated by a Telugu vowel.

  • Every feature of your phone is clever ways to push numbers around. Faster GPU equals more number crunching per second.
